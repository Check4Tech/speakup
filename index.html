About

Tim Ayrovainen has spent more than 35 years in special education. Hundreds of Committee on Special Education (CSE) meetings have included service both as an advocate and as an evaluating and treating member of a child’s educational team. From those meetings comes knowledge of how the CSE works and how the process secures services when they are appropriate.

Educational, medical, and clinical speech-language pathology, with positions nationally in schools and in medical settings, make up the range of the work. The current practice is a family-centered private practice in advocacy and speech-language pathology. The goal is to help parents and children find what will benefit the child.

Supervision has included graduate students from numerous universities. Consultation with families and with school districts covers plans for students with special learning needs.

New York State certification as a teacher of the speech/language impaired and licensure as a speech-language pathologist stand with undergraduate and master’s degrees in education and speech-language pathology, professional-level (PD) study in school district administration, and the Certificate of Clinical Competence (CCC) from the American Speech-Language-Hearing Association.

Services

The practice offers two kinds of help.

Advocacy

  • Consultation with families.
  • Review of current educational data to assess precisely how the student is performing, and of the documentation that supports growth. This is an educational and IEP audit.
  • Guidance for families in initiating evaluation processes.
  • Coaching to prepare for a CSE meeting.
  • Direct advocacy: review of academic performance and evaluation reports, attendance at IEP meetings, support in goal formulation, progress monitoring, and data review.
  • Indirect advocacy: information and guidance so families can manage their child’s educational program.

Speech-language pathology

Evaluation and treatment of communication delays and disorders.

This is speech therapy, and the evaluation that comes before it. An evaluation looks at how your child understands language and how your child speaks. Therapy follows what the evaluation shows and what you see at home and at school.

FAQ

A parent is a child’s first teacher. You know your child across home, school, and the rest of the day. You notice when something is not quite right. That might be health, mood, eating, or learning.

I offer these questions so you can look steadily at what you already see.

Do you feel that your child is struggling as a learner but you are unsure you understand why or how?

Answer this one for yourself. A yes, a no, or not sure is a complete answer.

Are you always assured that everything is fine, but you feel that it isn’t? Does this cycle repeat each year?

Answer this one for yourself. A yes, a no, or not sure is a complete answer.

Do you receive report cards that are inconsistent with the performance (better or worse) you observe on a regular basis?

Answer this one for yourself. A yes, a no, or not sure is a complete answer.

Are you confident that your child is receiving all available or appropriate academic support that can maximize their learning potential?

Answer this one for yourself. A yes, a no, or not sure is a complete answer.

Has your child been thoroughly assessed in a way that identifies both strengths and potential weaknesses as a learner?

Answer this one for yourself. A yes, a no, or not sure is a complete answer.

Is your child performing, or remaining, below grade-level expectations?

Answer this one for yourself. A yes, a no, or not sure is a complete answer.

Is your child receiving academic support, but nothing seems to measurably improve?

Answer this one for yourself. A yes, a no, or not sure is a complete answer.

Are you tired of paying for tutors without seeing direct measurable changes in performance?

Answer this one for yourself. A yes, a no, or not sure is a complete answer.

Does your child have an Individualized Education Plan (IEP), but still not achieving measurable progress to close gaps in learning?

Answer this one for yourself. A yes, a no, or not sure is a complete answer.

Do you feel your questions don’t always get answered to your satisfaction, or that your concerns aren’t getting the attention you think they deserve?

Answer this one for yourself. A yes, a no, or not sure is a complete answer.

Your answers can help you decide whether an educational advocate should join your child’s learning team.

About

Tim Ayrovainen

I have spent more than 35 years in special education, including hundreds of Committee on Special Education (CSE) meetings as an advocate and as an evaluating and treating member of a child’s educational team. In that time, I became familiar with the CSE process and the way services are secured when they are appropriate. I have supervised graduate students from numerous universities and consult with families and school districts on plans for students with special learning needs.

My work has taken place in schools and medical settings across the country, covering educational, medical, and clinical speech-language pathology. My work now is a family-centered private practice in advocacy and speech-language pathology. My goal is to help parents and children find the best path of success for their child.

My formal study included undergraduate and master’s degrees in education and speech-language pathology and professional-level (PD) study in school district administration. My clinical credentials include New York State certification as a teacher of the speech/language impaired, a license as a speech-language pathologist, and the Certificate of Clinical Competence (CCC) from the American Speech-Language-Hearing Association.

Services

The practice offers two kinds of help: advocacy around IEP and CSE meetings, and speech-language evaluation and therapy.

Advocacy

  • Consultation with families.
  • Review of current educational data to assess precisely how the student is performing, and of the documentation that supports growth. This is an educational and IEP audit.
  • Guidance for families in initiating evaluation processes.
  • Coaching to prepare for a CSE meeting.
  • Direct advocacy: review of academic performance and evaluation reports, attendance at IEP meetings, support in goal formulation, progress monitoring, and data review.
  • Indirect advocacy: information and guidance so families can manage their child’s educational program.

Speech-language pathology

Evaluation and treatment of communication delays and disorders.

This is speech therapy, and the evaluation that comes before it. An evaluation looks at how your child understands language and how your child speaks. Therapy follows what the evaluation shows and what you see at home and at school.

Questionnaire

I offer these questions so you can look steadily at what you already see.
